What is a Large Language Model?

A Large Language Model (LLM) is a type of artificial intelligence system trained on massive amounts of text data to understand and generate human language. These models use transformer architectures, which rely on self-attention mechanisms to process sequences of text.

Key characteristics of LLMs:
1. Scale: Modern LLMs have billions to trillions of parameters.
2. Pre-training: They are first trained on broad corpora (e.g., CommonCrawl, Wikipedia, books) to learn general language patterns.
3. Fine-tuning: They are then specialized for specific tasks through instruction tuning or reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
4. Emergent abilities: At sufficient scale, LLMs exhibit capabilities not explicitly trained, such as few-shot learning and reasoning.

Notable examples include GPT-4, Claude, LLaMA, and Gemini. LLMs power chatbots, code assistants, search tools, and creative writing applications.
